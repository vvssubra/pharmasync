# B9. Otorhinolaryngology Infections

## 1. Tonsillitis / Pharyngitis (Group A Streptococcus)
- **Preferred**
  - Phenoxymethylpenicillin (penicillin V) 25–50mg/kg/day PO in 4 divided doses (max. 2g/day)
  - OR Amoxicillin 50mg/kg/day PO in 1 or 2 divided doses (max. 1g/day)
  - OR \*Benzylpenicillin 100,000–200,000 units/kg IV in 4 divided doses (max. 24 million units/day)
  - Switch to oral (penicillin V / amoxicillin) when improving and tolerating oral. Total 10 days.
- **Alternative (non-life-threatening antibiotic allergy)**
  - Cephalexin 25–50mg/kg/day PO in 2 divided doses (max. 2g/day)
  - OR Erythromycin ethylsuccinate 40–50mg/kg/day PO in 2 divided doses (max. 800mg/dose). 10 days.
- **Comments:** Once-daily amoxicillin (50mg/kg; max 1g/day) for 10 days is as effective as penicillin V
  or multiple-dose amoxicillin (endorsed by AHA and IDSA for GAS pharyngitis and ARF prevention);
  adherence is important with once-daily dosing. \*IV is not routinely recommended for uncomplicated
  tonsillopharyngitis; consider in severe cases or if oral is not tolerated.

## 2. Rhinosinusitis
Most commonly viral. **Bacteria:** S. pneumoniae; H. influenzae; M. catarrhalis; Group A Streptococcus.
- **Preferred:** Amoxicillin 80–90mg/kg/day (max. 2g/day) PO for 5 days in 2 divided doses
- **Alternative:** Non-life-threatening allergy: Cefuroxime 30mg/kg/day (max. 1g/day) PO for 5 days in 2
  divided doses. Severe allergy: Erythromycin ethylsuccinate 40–50mg/kg/day (max. 800mg/dose) PO for 5
  days in 2 divided doses.
- **Comments:** Suspect acute bacterial sinusitis in a child with URI who has (1) persistent illness
  (nasal discharge and/or daytime cough ≥10 days without improvement), (2) worsening course, or
  (3) severe onset (fever with purulent discharge for 3 days). Most experts use high-dose amoxicillin
  (90mg/kg/day).

## 3. Acute Otitis Media
**Organisms:** S. pneumoniae; H. influenzae; M. catarrhalis.
- **Preferred:** Amoxicillin 80–90mg/kg/day (max. 1g/dose) PO in 2–3 divided doses.
  For clinical failure, amoxicillin use in the last 30 days, or concurrent purulent conjunctivitis:
  - Suspension 14:1: Amoxicillin/clavulanate 80–90mg/kg/day PO in 2 divided doses (amoxicillin component, max. 3g/day)
  - Suspension 7:1: Amoxicillin/clavulanate 40–50mg/kg/day PO in 2 divided doses (amoxicillin component, max. 3g/day)
- **Alternative:** Non-life-threatening allergy: Cefuroxime 30mg/kg/day (max. 1g/day) PO in 2 divided
  doses. Severe allergy: Erythromycin ethylsuccinate 40–50mg/kg/day PO (max. 800mg/dose) in 2 divided
  doses; OR Azithromycin 10mg/kg/dose PO Day 1 (max. 500mg/day), then 5mg/kg/dose PO q24h Days 2–5 (max. 250mg/day).
- **Comments:** Most uncomplicated AOM needs no antibiotics, except: children <2 years;
  immunosuppression; severe disease. Duration: <2 years 7–10 days; >2 years 5–7 days. The 14:1
  preparation (90mg/kg/day amoxicillin with 6.4mg/kg/day clavulanate, 2 divided doses) is less likely
  to cause diarrhoea. Amoxicillin/clavulanate 40–50mg/kg/day may be inadequate for penicillin-
  nonsusceptible S. pneumoniae.

## 4. Acute Otitis Externa
**Organisms:** P. aeruginosa; S. aureus.
- **Preferred (mild–moderate):** Topical antibiotic ± topical steroid, e.g.:
  - Gentamicin 0.3% ear drops 3–4 drops 3 times/day for 7 days
  - Polymyxin B sulphate 10,000U / neomycin sulphate 5mg / hydrocortisone 10g ear drops: 4 drops 3 or 4 times/day for 7 days
  - Ofloxacin 0.3% otic solution 5 drops into affected ear(s) once daily for 7 days (for ages 1–12 years)
- **Comments:** Consult ORL surgeon. Do not use ototoxic agents (gentamicin, neomycin) with
  tympanostomy tubes or a perforated tympanic membrane. Response expected in 48–72 hours; full
  response may take up to 6 days. Non-response → evaluate for obstruction, foreign body, non-adherence,
  or alternative diagnosis.

## 5. Diphtheria (Corynebacterium diphtheriae)
- **Preferred:** Diphtheria antitoxin (dose per the source's table — not included in the pasted text)
  PLUS Azithromycin 10mg/kg/day IV/PO q24h (max. 500mg/dose) for 14 days; OR Benzylpenicillin
  100,000–150,000 units/kg/day in 4 divided doses (max. 1 MU/dose), then Phenoxymethylpenicillin
  50mg/kg/day PO in 4 divided doses (max. 500mg/dose), total 14 days (IV + oral).
- **Alternative:** Diphtheria antitoxin PLUS Erythromycin 40–50mg/kg/day IV/PO in 4 divided doses
  (max. IV 500mg/dose; PO 800mg/dose) for 14 days.
- **Comments:** Antibiotics do not replace antitoxin; their role is to eradicate the organism and stop
  toxin production. Parenteral if unable to swallow; switch to oral once improved (Appendix 6).
- **Close contact prophylaxis:** IM Benzathine penicillin (BW <30kg 600,000 U; BW >30kg 1.2 MU single
  dose); OR Erythromycin ethylsuccinate 40–50mg/kg/day PO q6h for 7–10 days; OR Azithromycin
  10mg/kg/day PO OD for 7–10 days.

## 6. Acute Epiglottitis
- **Preferred:** Ceftriaxone 50–75mg/kg/dose IV daily (max. 2g/dose); OR Cefotaxime 150–200mg/kg/day
  (max. 4g/day) IV in 4 divided doses
- **Alternative:** Clindamycin 30–40mg/kg/day IV (max. 1.8g/day) in 3 divided doses
- **Comments:** 7–10 days. Switch to oral (e.g. amoxicillin/clavulanate) when improving and tolerating oral.

# B10. Respiratory Infections (Lower Respiratory Tract)

## 1. Community-Acquired Pneumonia (CAP)

### 1.1 Outpatient (infant ≥3 months and children)
Viral causes are more common (influenza, RSV, hMPV, parainfluenza, adenovirus). **Bacteria:**
S. pneumoniae; Group A Streptococcus; S. aureus; H. influenzae.

- **Preferred:** \*High-dose amoxicillin 80–90mg/kg/day PO in 2–3 divided doses (max. 1g/dose, 3g/day) for 5 days.
  - Oral weight bands: 3–5kg 250mg q12h; 6–9kg 375mg q12h; 10–14kg 500mg q12h; 15–19kg 750mg q12h;
    >20kg 500mg q8h or 1g q12h.
  - \*Either weight-band or mg/kg dosing may be used. WHO endorses weight bands to streamline
    prescribing and reduce errors; higher doses from band ranges are considered safe given
    amoxicillin's wide therapeutic index.
  - **Influenza (if suspected, start early) — Oseltamivir for 5 days:** <9 months 3mg/kg PO q12h;
    9–11 months 3.5mg/kg PO q12h; 1–12 years: ≤15kg 30mg q12h; >15–23kg 45mg q12h; >23–40kg 60mg q12h; >40kg 75mg q12h.
- **Alternative:** Erythromycin ethylsuccinate 40–50mg/kg/day PO in 2 divided doses (max. 800mg/dose)
- **Comments:** Antibiotics not routinely recommended since viral infection is more common; children
  admitted to hospital are treated as presumed bacterial unless viral origin is known. Use a macrolide
  if mycoplasma or chlamydia pneumonia is suspected (may be started in school-aged children). Duration:
  minimum 5 days and until afebrile 2–3 days (empiric therapy without identified aetiology; pneumococcus,
  H. influenzae, M. catarrhalis). Children not responding to oral amoxicillin (at least 40mg/kg/dose)
  should be referred to a centre with second-line treatment. High-dose amoxicillin overcomes
  pneumococcal cell-wall resistance. Standard dose (amoxicillin 45–50mg/kg/day PO in 3 divided doses,
  max. 500mg/dose, 5 days) only for children who cannot tolerate high doses.

### 1.2 Inpatient (fully immunised)
- **Preferred:** Benzylpenicillin 150,000–200,000 units/kg/day IV in 3–4 divided doses (max. 24 million units/day), 5–7 days
- **Alternative (second line / partially treated):** Cefuroxime 100–150mg/kg/day IV in 3 divided doses
  (max. 6g/day); OR Amoxicillin/clavulanate \*30mg/kg/dose IV q8h (max. 1.2g/dose)
- **Comments:** Macrolide if mycoplasma/chlamydia suspected. \*Amoxicillin dosing for the 5:1 formulation.

## 2. Severe CAP (child not fully immunised / life-threatening)
- **Preferred:** Cefotaxime 150–200mg/kg/day in 3–4 divided doses (max. 8g/day); OR Ceftriaxone
  75–100mg/kg/day in 2 divided doses (max. 4g/day). MAY ADD \*Azithromycin 10mg/kg/dose (max. 500mg) IV
  q24h Day 1, then 5mg/kg/dose (max. 250mg) Days 2–5, if atypical organisms are considered.
- **Alternative:** Cefuroxime 75–150mg/kg/day IV in 3 divided doses (max. 6g/day); MAY ADD \*Azithromycin as above.
- **Comments:** 5–7 days; longer and assess for complications such as empyema if not fully recovered
  (see Empyema thoracis). \*The role of antimicrobials for M. pneumoniae / C. pneumoniae is unknown
  (most resolve without macrolides); may hasten recovery in seriously ill children or persistent cough.
  Macrolide resistance in M. pneumoniae is now very common in Asia — avoid routine use.

## 3. Empyema Thoracis
**Organisms:** S. aureus; S. pneumoniae. Others: S. pyogenes; H. influenzae and other gram-negatives in
immunocompromised patients. If not responding, rule out TB.

- **Preferred:** Cefuroxime 100–200mg/kg/day IV in 3 divided doses (max. 6g/day); OR Cefotaxime
  200–300mg/kg/day IV in 4 divided doses (max. 8g/day); PLUS Cloxacillin 200–300mg/kg/day IV in 4–6
  divided doses (max. 12g/day). Duration 4–6 weeks.
- **Alternative**
  - MSSA: Cloxacillin 200–300mg/kg/day IV in 4–6 divided doses (max. 12g/day) for 4–6 weeks
  - Penicillin-sensitive S. pneumoniae: Benzylpenicillin 200,000–300,000 units/kg/day IV in 4–6 divided doses (max. 24 million units/day)
  - Penicillin-resistant S. pneumoniae (use C&S): Cefotaxime 200–300mg/kg/day IV in 4 divided doses (max. 8g/day); OR Ceftriaxone 100mg/kg/day IV in 1–2 divided doses (max. 4g/day)
- **Comments:** Base on C&S of pleural fluid/tissue or blood culture. All children need high-dose IV
  therapy for pleural penetration. Pneumatocele suggests S. aureus but also occurs in pneumococcal
  disease. No consensus on duration — most recommend 3–6 weeks total by severity. For moderate–severe
  or stage 1–2 parapneumonic effusion, consider pleural drainage and intrapleural fibrinolytic. Stage 2
  failed medical therapy and stage 3 need surgery (Malaysian Paediatric Empyema Thoracis consensus
  guideline, 2020).

# B11. Skin & Soft Tissue Infections

Note: oral doses marked \* below are for children <25kg; for >25kg use the adult dose
(500mg PO q6h), per the source.

## 1. Abscess (S. aureus)
- **Preferred**
  - Mild: \*Cloxacillin 50–100mg/kg/day PO in 4 divided doses (max. 2g/day), 5–7 days
  - Severe: Cloxacillin 200mg/kg/day IV in 4 divided doses (max. 12g/day), 5–7 days
  - CA-MRSA: Clindamycin 30–40mg/kg/day PO in 3–4 divided doses (max. 2.7g/day); OR
    Trimethoprim/sulfamethoxazole 8–12mg/kg/day (TMP) PO in 2 divided doses (max. 320mg/day); 5–7 days
- **Alternative (mild):** Cephalexin 25–50mg/kg/day PO in 2 divided doses (max. 2g/day), 5–7 days
- **Comments:** Incision & drainage is the mainstay; send pus from I&D for C&S if needle aspiration is
  inadequate. Parenteral for severe infection. Consider CA-MRSA if poorly resolving (generally still uncommon).

## 2. Cellulitis (S. aureus; S. pyogenes)
- **Preferred:** Mild: \*Cloxacillin 50–100mg/kg/day PO in 4 divided doses (max. 2g/day), 5–7 days.
  Severe: Cloxacillin 200mg/kg/day IV in 4 divided doses (max. 12g/day), 5–7 days.
- **Alternative:** Cephalexin 25–50mg/kg/day PO in 2 divided doses (max. 2g/day), 5–7 days
- **Comments:** Parenteral for extensive lesions. Treat until 3 days after acute inflammation resolves.

## 3. Hansen's Disease / Leprosy (M. leprae)
- **Preferred**
  - <10 years or <40kg: Rifampicin 10mg/kg PO (max. 600mg) once monthly; PLUS Dapsone 2mg/kg PO q24h
    (max. 100mg/day); PLUS Clofazimine 100mg PO once monthly and 50mg twice weekly
  - 10–14 years and >40kg: Rifampicin 450mg PO (max. 600mg) once monthly; PLUS Dapsone 50mg PO q24h
    (max. 100mg/day); PLUS Clofazimine 150mg PO once monthly and 50mg on alternate days
- **Comments:** Paucibacillary: 6 months, surveillance 5 years. Multibacillary: 12 months, surveillance 15 years.

## 4. Impetigo (S. aureus; S. pyogenes)
- **Preferred:** Localised: topical 2% fusidic acid 2–3 times daily for 7 days (outpatient).
  Generalised: \*Cloxacillin 50–100mg/kg/day PO in 4 divided doses (max. 1g/day), 5–7 days.
- **Alternative:** Localised: topical 2% mupirocin cream 2–3 times daily for 7 days.
  Generalised: Cephalexin 25–50mg/kg/day PO in 2 divided doses (max. 2g/day), 5–7 days.

## 5. Necrotising Fasciitis
**Organisms:** Group A Streptococcus and other streptococci; S. aureus (MSSA and CA-MRSA); also consider
Clostridia and polymicrobial infection.

### 5.1 Streptococcal
- **Preferred:** Benzylpenicillin 200,000–300,000 units/kg/day IV in 4–6 divided doses (max. 24 million
  units/day) PLUS Clindamycin 30–40mg/kg/day IV in 3–4 divided doses (max. 2.7g/day)
- **Comments:** ~50% have streptococcal toxic shock syndrome. Prompt, aggressive surgical debridement
  is the mainstay. Send tissue for Gram stain and culture. Clindamycin is needed in combination to block
  toxin production whether or not toxic shock is present. IVIG adjunct in group A streptococcal
  infection: 1g/kg Day 1, then 0.5g/kg on 1–2 subsequent days. Duration: at least 2 weeks if no focus
  (no deep-seated, heart, bone or joint involvement).

### 5.2 Staphylococcal
- **Preferred:** Cloxacillin 200mg/kg/day IV in 4–6 divided doses (max. 2g/dose, 12g/day) PLUS
  Clindamycin 30–40mg/kg/day IV in 3–4 divided doses (max. 2.7g/day)
- **Alternative (CA-MRSA suspected):** Vancomycin 60mg/kg/day IV in 3–4 divided doses (max. 2g/day)
- **Comments:** As 5.1 for debridement, tissue culture and clindamycin. Vancomycin is NOT recommended for
  serious MSSA infection (inferior outcomes versus anti-staphylococcal β-lactam; limits vancomycin
  resistance). Duration at least 2 weeks if no focus.

## 6. Staphylococcal Scalded Skin Syndrome (S. aureus)
- **Preferred:** Cloxacillin 200mg/kg/day IV in 4–6 divided doses (max. 12g/day); step down to
  Cloxacillin 50–100mg/kg/day PO in 4 divided doses (max. 2g/day). Total 7–10 days.
- **Alternative:** Cefazolin 100mg/kg/day IV in 3–4 divided doses (max. 6g/day); step down to Cephalexin
  50mg/kg/day PO in 2 divided doses (max. 2g/day)
- **Comments:** Switch to oral when improving and blood culture is negative. Doses are for <25kg; use
  adult dose above 25kg.

## 7. Animal Bites
Consider rabies prophylaxis per local epidemiology. Other animal bites: discuss with ID physician.

### 7.1 Cat bites
**Organisms:** Pasteurella multocida; Staphylococcus; Streptococcus; Neisseria; Moraxella.
- **Preferred:** Amoxicillin/clavulanate, 5–7 days — suspension 14:1: 80–90mg/kg/day PO in 2 divided
  doses (amoxicillin component, max. 2g/day); suspension 7:1: 40–50mg/kg/day PO in 2 divided doses
  (amoxicillin component, max. 2g/day)
- **Alternative:** Amoxicillin/clavulanate 30mg/kg/dose (amoxicillin component) IV q8h (max. 1.2g/dose)

### 7.2 Dog bites
**Organisms:** Pasteurella canis; Staphylococcus; Streptococcus; Fusobacterium.
- **Preferred:** Amoxicillin/clavulanate as in 7.1, 5–7 days
- **Alternative:** Amoxicillin/clavulanate 30mg/kg/dose (amoxicillin component) IV q8h (max. 1.2g/dose);
  OR Clindamycin 20–40mg/kg/day IV in 3 divided doses (max. 2.7g/day) PLUS Co-trimoxazole 8–10mg
  (TMP)/kg/day IV in 2 divided doses (max. 320mg TMP/day)

## 8. Scabies
- **Preferred**
  - Infant <2 months: Sulphur 6% in petroleum ointment base for 3 days
  - Children <2 years: two applications of permethrin 5% for 8–12 hours, one week apart
  - Children <12 years: two applications of permethrin 5% for 8–12 hours, one week apart
  - Nodular scabies: <2 years Crotamiton cream TDS 5–7 days; <12 years Crotamiton cream TDS 7–14 days
- **Alternative:** <2 years: Sulphur 6% in petroleum ointment base for 3 days. <12 years: Benzyl
  benzoate 12.5%, whole body neck down, for 3 consecutive days.
- **Comments:** Treat the whole body including the face (avoid eyes and mouth). Treat all family
  members/close contacts at the same time. See the MOH Guideline for Management of Scabies in Adults
  and Children (2015) for cautions and application.

## 9. Tinea Capitis (Trichophyton, Microsporum)
- **Preferred:** Griseofulvin 10–20mg/kg/day PO daily (max. 750mg/day) for at least 6 weeks until clinically clear
- **Alternative:** Terbinafine 4–6mg/kg/day PO daily (max. 250mg/day) for 4 weeks or longer by
  pathogen (10–20kg 62.5mg daily; 20–40kg 125mg daily; >40kg 250mg daily); OR Itraconazole 3–5mg/kg/day
  PO daily (max. 200mg/day) for 2–6 weeks; OR Fluconazole 5–6mg/kg/day PO (max. 300mg/day) for 3–6 weeks
- **Comments:** Griseofulvin is first line for Microsporum; take with fatty food; monitor LFTs if >8
  weeks. Adjunctive selenium or ketoconazole shampoo can reduce transmissibility.

# B13. Urinary Tract Infections

## 1. Acute Pyelonephritis
**Organisms:** E. coli; Proteus; Klebsiella; Enterobacter.

- **Preferred — 0–2 months old**
  - First line: Ampicillin 50mg/kg/dose IV (≤1 week of age q12h; >1 week q8h) PLUS Gentamicin
    5mg/kg/dose IV (≤30 weeks CGA q48h; >30–34 weeks CGA q36h; >35 weeks CGA q24h)
  - Second line: Cefotaxime 150–200mg/kg/day IV in 3 divided doses (max. 6g/day); MAY ADD Amikacin 15mg/kg/dose IV daily
- **Preferred — ≥3 months old**
  - First line: Cephalexin 25–50mg/kg/day PO in 2 divided doses (max. 2g/day); OR Amoxicillin/clavulanate
    — suspension 14:1: 80–90mg/kg/day PO in 2 divided doses (amoxicillin component, max. 2g/day);
    suspension 7:1: 40–50mg/kg/day PO in 2 divided doses (amoxicillin component, max. 2g/day)
  - Second line: Cefotaxime 150–200mg/kg/day IV in 3 divided doses (max. 6g/day); OR Ceftriaxone
    100mg/kg/day IV in 1–2 divided doses (max. 2g/day); MAY ADD Amikacin 15mg/kg/dose IV daily
- **Comments:** 7–10 days. Adjust per culture. Switch to oral when improving and tolerating oral. For
  gentamicin in obese patients, calculate the parenteral dose on ideal weight for height and monitor
  serum levels closely.

## 2. Lower Urinary Tract Infection
- **Preferred:** Amoxicillin/clavulanate — suspension 14:1: 80–90mg/kg/day PO in 2 divided doses
  (amoxicillin component, max. 2g/day); suspension 7:1: 40–50mg/kg/day PO in 2 divided doses
  (amoxicillin component, max. 2g/day); OR Cefuroxime 30mg/kg/day PO in 2 divided doses (max. 500mg/day,
  as printed); OR Trimethoprim/sulfamethoxazole 8–10mg/kg/day (TMP) PO in 2 divided doses
- **Alternative:** Nitrofurantoin 2mg/kg/dose PO q12h (sustained release) or 1mg/kg/dose q6h
  (immediate-release tablets); max. 100mg/dose
- **Comments:** 3–5 days.

## 3. Prophylaxis for UTI (infants and children with recurrent UTI)
- **Preferred:** Trimethoprim 1–2mg/kg PO at night (max. 100mg ON); OR Trimethoprim/sulfamethoxazole
  2mg/kg (TMP) PO at night; OR Nitrofurantoin 1–2mg/kg PO at night (max. 100mg ON)
- **Alternative:** Cephalexin 12.5mg/kg PO at night (max. 250mg/dose)
- **Comments:** Not routinely recommended after a first UTI. Prophylactic antibiotics for 3 days with
  MCUG on the second day. In children with grade I–IV vesicoureteral reflux, recurrent UTI may fall by
  50% after a first or second febrile/symptomatic UTI, with more resistant organisms detected after prophylaxis.
