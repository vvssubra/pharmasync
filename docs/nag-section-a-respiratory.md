## 1. Community Acquired Pneumonia (CAP)

**General notes**
- Diagnosis generally needs an infiltrate on chest radiograph plus a compatible syndrome
  (fever, dyspnoea, cough, sputum).
- Obtain previous healthcare encounters and antibiotic exposure.
- Consider and investigate pulmonary TB when symptoms are prolonged, risk factors are present,
  or radiology is compatible.
- Choose antibiotics by likely aetiology and local antibiogram where possible.
- CURB-65 / SMART-COP support, not replace, clinical judgment.

### Severity scores

**CURB-65** (1 point each): new Confusion; Urea >7 mmol/l; RR ≥30/min; BP ≤90/60 mmHg; Age ≥65
- 0–1 mild, outpatient · 2 moderate, consider admission · 3–5 severe, admit, consider ICU
- CRB-65 (no urea) may be used; interpretation is the same.

**SMART-COP**
- Systolic BP <90 (2) · Multilobar CXR (1) · Albumin <3.5 g/dL (1)
- RR ≥30/min, or ≥25/min if age ≤50 (1) · HR ≥125/min (1) · New confusion (1)
- Oxygenation (2): age >50 — SpO2 ≤90% or PaO2 <60 or P/F <250; age ≤50 — SpO2 ≤93% or PaO2 <70 or P/F <333
- Arterial pH <7.35 (2)
- 0–2 mild, outpatient · 3–4 moderate, admit · ≥5 severe, consider ICU

### 1.1 Outpatient
- **Preferred:** Amoxicillin 500mg–1g PO q8h, 5–7 days
- **Alternative:** Doxycycline 100mg PO q12h, 5–7 days

### 1.2 Outpatient with comorbidities
- **Preferred:** Amoxicillin/clavulanate 625mg PO q8h, 5–7 days
  - MAY ADD \*Azithromycin 500mg PO q24h for 3 days OR \*Doxycycline 100mg PO q12h for 5–7 days
- **Alternative (antibiotic allergy):** \*\*Levofloxacin 750mg PO q24h, 5 days
- **Comments**
  - Comorbidities: chronic heart/lung/liver/renal disease, diabetes, alcoholism, malignancy, asplenia.
  - \*If atypical pathogen suspected (extrapulmonary manifestations, diffuse infiltrates).
  - \*\*Levofloxacin strictly reserved for antibiotic allergy (higher adverse-event risk).

### 1.3 Inpatient — moderate (CURB-65 = 2 or SMART-COP 3–4)
- **Preferred:** Amoxicillin/clavulanate 1.2g IV q8h, 5–7 days
  - MAY ADD \*Azithromycin 500mg IV/PO q24h for 3 days OR \*Doxycycline 100mg PO q12h for 5–7 days
- **Alternative:** Ceftriaxone 2g IV q24h, 5–7 days
  - MAY ADD \*Azithromycin 500mg IV/PO q24h for 3 days
- **Antibiotic allergy:** \*\*Levofloxacin 750mg IV/PO q24h, 5–7 days
- **Comments**
  - \*Atypical pathogen suspected; longer duration may be needed in immunocompromised patients or confirmed Legionella.
  - \*\*Levofloxacin strictly reserved for antibiotic allergy.
  - Switch to oral when improved and tolerating oral.
  - Suspected melioidosis → Tropical Infections section.

### 1.4 Inpatient — severe (CURB-65 ≥3 or SMART-COP ≥5)
- **Preferred:** Amoxicillin/clavulanate 1.2g IV q8h, 5–7 days
  - MAY ADD \*Azithromycin 500mg IV/PO q24h for 3 days OR \*Doxycycline 100mg PO q12h for 5–7 days
- **Alternative:** Ceftriaxone 2g IV q24h, 5–7 days
- **If at risk of pseudomonal infection:** \*\*Piperacillin/tazobactam 4.5g IV q6–8h for 7 days
  OR Cefepime 2g IV q8h for 7 days; MAY ADD \*Azithromycin 500mg IV/PO q24h for 3 days
- **Strong pseudomonal risk factors:** prior respiratory infection/isolation of P. aeruginosa;
  hospitalisation with IV antibiotics in past 3 months; gram-negative rods on good-quality sputum Gram stain
- **Other pseudomonal risk factors:** recent hospitalisation or long-term care; recent antibiotic use;
  frequent COPD exacerbations needing steroids/antibiotics; structural lung disease (e.g. bronchiectasis);
  immunosuppression
- **Comments**
  - \*Atypical pathogen suspected; longer duration for immunocompromised/Legionella.
  - \*\*Piperacillin/tazobactam if given q8h: extended infusion over 3–4 hours.
  - IV ceftazidime not recommended empirically (poor S. pneumoniae activity).
  - Suspected melioidosis → Tropical Infections section.

## 2. Viral Pneumonia

### 2.1 COVID-19
Refer to the MOH COVID-19 Management Guidelines (Annex 2, 8 April 2024).

### 2.2 Influenza
- **Preferred:** Oseltamivir 75mg PO q12h, 5 days
- **Alternative:** \*Baloxavir — 40–80kg: 40mg PO single dose; ≥80kg: 80mg PO single dose
- **Comments:** Greatest benefit if started within 48 hours. In hospitalised/immunocompromised
  patients, treat regardless of symptom duration; may need longer course.
  \*Not in the MOH Drug Formulary.

### 2.3 Varicella zoster
- **Preferred:** Acyclovir 10mg/kg IV q8h, 7 days

## 3. Lung Abscess and Empyema

### 3.1 Empirical treatment

**Preferred**
- No recent thoracic surgery/procedure or HAP/VAP: Amoxicillin/clavulanate 1.2g IV q6–8h
  OR Ampicillin/sulbactam 3g IV q6h
- Post-procedure (pleural interventions, thoracic/oesophageal surgery) or HAP/VAP:
  \*Piperacillin-tazobactam 4.5g IV q6–8h OR Cefepime 2g IV q8h PLUS Metronidazole 500mg IV q8h
- MAY ADD \*\*Vancomycin 15–20mg/kg (actual body weight) IV q8–12h; max 2g/dose

**Alternative**
- No recent surgery/procedure or HAP/VAP: Ceftriaxone 2g IV q24h PLUS Metronidazole 500mg IV q8h
- Antibiotic allergy: Clindamycin 600mg IV/PO q8h
- Post-procedure or HAP/VAP: Meropenem 1g IV q8h; MAY ADD \*\*Vancomycin 15–20mg/kg IV q8–12h

**Comments**
- Duration: adequate source control 2–4 weeks from drainage/surgery and defervescence;
  inadequate source control 4–6 weeks.
- Empyema: attempt drainage.
- Change to oral once improved (Appendix 6). Avoid aminoglycosides in empyema.
- \*Pip/tazo q8h: extended infusion over 3–4 hours.
- \*\*MRSA cover per local hospital MRSA prevalence; vancomycin dosing per Appendix 1.
- Suspected melioidosis → Tropical Infections; antibiotic allergy → Appendix 3.

### 3.2 MSSA
- **Preferred:** Cloxacillin 2g IV q4h
- **Alternative:** Cefazolin 2g IV q8h
- **Comments:** 4–6 weeks depending on response; step down to oral (e.g. amoxicillin/clavulanate
  625mg PO q8h or cephalexin 1g PO q6h) once stable and improved.

## 4. Infective Exacerbation of COPD

Antibiotics only if increased sputum purulence AND (increased sputum volume OR increased
dyspnoea), OR the patient is intubated.

### 4.1 Outpatient
- **Preferred:** Amoxicillin/clavulanate 625mg PO q8h, 5 days
- **Alternative:** Cefuroxime 500mg PO q12h, 5 days OR Doxycycline 100mg PO q12h, 5 days

### 4.2 Inpatient
- **Preferred:** Amoxicillin/clavulanate 1.2g IV q8h, 5–7 days; MAY ADD Azithromycin 500mg IV/PO, 3 days
- **Alternative:** Ceftriaxone 2g IV q24h, 5–7 days; MAY ADD Azithromycin 500mg IV/PO, 3 days

### 4.3 Inpatient with pseudomonal risk factors
- **Preferred:** \*Piperacillin-tazobactam 4.5g IV q6–8h, 7 days; MAY ADD Azithromycin 500mg IV/PO, 3 days
- **Alternative:** Cefepime 2g IV q8h, 7 days; MAY ADD Azithromycin 500mg IV/PO, 3 days
- **Risk factors:** chronic colonisation/previous sputum isolation of P. aeruginosa; very severe COPD
  (FEV1 <30%); bronchiectasis; broad-spectrum antibiotics within 3 months; chronic systemic steroids
- \*Pip/tazo q8h: extended infusion over 3–4 hours.

## 5. Hospital Acquired Pneumonia (HAP/VAP)

- **HAP:** ≥48 hours after admission, not incubating at admission. **VAP:** ≥48 hours after intubation.
- **MDR risk factors (HAP/VAP):** IV antibiotics within 90 days; prior MDR colonisation/infection.
- **Additional MDR risk factors (VAP):** septic shock at VAP; ARDS or acute RRT before VAP; ≥5 days
  hospitalisation before VAP.

### 5.1 Early onset (2–4 days after admission/intubation)
- **Preferred:** Amoxicillin/clavulanate 1.2g IV q8h, 5–7 days
- **Alternative:** Ceftriaxone 2g IV q24h, 5–7 days
- **Comments:** Adjust to local antibiogram; consider broader spectrum if deteriorating on adequate therapy.

### 5.2 Late onset (≥5 days) OR severe pneumonia OR pseudomonal risk
- **Preferred:** \*Piperacillin-tazobactam 4.5g IV q6–8h, 7 days OR Cefepime 2g IV q8h, 7 days
- **Alternative (high MDR risk):** Meropenem 1g IV q8h, 7 days OR Imipenem/cilastatin 500mg IV q6h, 7 days
- **Comments**
  - See CAP section for pseudomonal risk.
  - Severe pneumonia: septic shock; respiratory failure needing ventilatory support; rapid CXR progression.
  - Empirical cover depends on local prevalence; de-escalate by culture and sensitivity.
  - Longer duration may be indicated clinically/radiologically/biochemically.
  - \*Pip/tazo q8h: extended infusion over 3–4 hours.

## 6. Aspiration Pneumonia

Distinguish aspiration pneumonia (bacterial infection from oropharyngeal organisms) from chemical
pneumonitis (acid injury; **no antibiotic indicated**).

**Antibiotics indicated:** delayed symptoms (vs onset within hours in pneumonitis); patients on gastric
acid suppression or with bowel obstruction.

- **Preferred:** Amoxicillin/clavulanate 1.2g IV q8h
- **Alternative:** Ceftriaxone 2g IV q24h; MAY ADD \*Metronidazole 500mg IV q8h
- **Antibiotic allergy:** Clindamycin 600mg IV/PO q8h
- **Comments:** Duration 7 days. \*Anaerobic cover not routinely needed unless lung abscess/empyema
  suspected. Switch to oral when improved and tolerating oral.
