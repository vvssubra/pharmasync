## 1. Asymptomatic Bacteriuria (ABU)
Urine growth ≥10^5 cfu/mL in 2 serial samples (women) or a single sample (men), without UTI symptoms.

- **Treatment is NOT indicated, unless:**
  - \*in pregnant women (5 days)
  - \*\*before TURP or urological procedures breaching the mucosa (3–5 days; follow-up culture
    before the procedure is not necessary)
- **Do not screen or treat ABU in:** women without risk factors; post-menopausal women; indwelling or
  suprapubic catheters and nephrostomy tubes; elderly institutionalised patients; dysfunctional or
  reconstructed lower urinary tracts; renal transplant; before arthroplasty; history of recurrent UTI.

## 2. Uncomplicated UTI
Infection confined to the bladder in afebrile men and women.
**Organisms:** E. coli; K. pneumoniae; S. agalactiae.

- **Preferred:** \*Nitrofurantoin 50–100mg PO q6h (macrocrystals, immediate release); OR Nitrofurantoin
  100mg PO q12h (monohydrate/macrocrystals, sustained release); OR Amoxicillin/clavulanate 625mg PO q8h;
  OR Ampicillin/sulbactam 375–750mg PO q12h
- **Alternative:** Cephalexin 500mg PO q12h; OR Cefuroxime 250–500mg PO q12h; OR \*\*Fosfomycin 3g PO x 1 dose
  (unlikely to work for gram-negatives other than E. coli — they may carry the FosA hydrolase gene)
- **Comments**
  - 3–5 days, counted from the first day of effective therapy.
  - \*Avoid nitrofurantoin if GFR <30 mL/min and in third-trimester pregnancy.
  - Trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole is not recommended empirically (local E. coli and Klebsiella
    resistance consistently >30%).
  - Do not use amoxicillin or ampicillin empirically (poor efficacy, very high resistance).
  - \*\*Confirmed ESBL E. coli sensitive to fosfomycin: give every 48 hours for a total of 3 doses.

## 3. Complicated UTI
**Organisms:** E. coli; K. pneumoniae; E. faecalis; S. agalactiae; P. aeruginosa.
Infection beyond the bladder: pyelonephritis; febrile or bacteraemic UTI; catheter-associated UTI
(CAUTI); prostatitis (see prostatitis section). Sepsis from complicated UTI → Urosepsis section.

- **Preferred:** Amoxicillin/clavulanate 1.2g IV q6–8h; OR Ampicillin/sulbactam 1.5–3g IV q6h;
  MAY ADD \*aminoglycoside
- **Alternative:** Cefuroxime 750–1500mg IV q8h; OR Ceftriaxone 2g IV q24h; OR \*Ceftazidime 2g IV q8h
- **Comments:** 5–7 days. \*Consider ceftazidime or adding an aminoglycoside (e.g. amikacin) if at risk
  of P. aeruginosa. Step down to oral by culture/sensitivity once afebrile >48 hours, improving and
  tolerating oral. Pyuria alone, without other symptoms, is not diagnostic of CAUTI and needs no treatment.

## 4. Urosepsis
Systemic UTI that may lead to organ dysfunction.
**Organisms:** E. coli; K. pneumoniae; E. faecalis; S. agalactiae; P. aeruginosa.
Early source control is the mainstay. Choose broad-spectrum therapy by severity of illness,
patient risk factors for resistant organisms, local antibiogram, and other factors (allergies,
immunocompromise, age).

- **Preferred**
  - Without shock: Ceftriaxone 2g IV q24h; OR Cefotaxime 2g IV q8h
  - With shock: \*Piperacillin-tazobactam 4.5g IV q6–8h
- **Alternative**
  - Without shock: Ceftazidime 2g IV q8h; OR Cefepime 2g IV q8h
  - With shock: \*\*Meropenem 1g IV q8h; OR \*\*Imipenem 500mg IV q6h
- **Comments:** 7 days or more. For urinary tract abscess or bacterial prostatitis see the respective
  sections. \*Pip/tazo q8h: extended infusion over 3–4 hours. \*\*Reserve carbapenems for MDR/ESBL risk.

## 5. Recurrent UTI
At least three UTIs/year, or two UTIs in the last six months.

### 5.1 Prophylaxis
- **Preferred:** Nitrofurantoin 50–100mg PO ON (macrocrystals); OR Nitrofurantoin 100mg PO ON
  (monohydrate/macrocrystals, dual release); OR Cephalexin 250mg PO ON
- **Alternative:** Trimethoprim/sulfamethoxazole 80/400mg PO ON; OR Trimethoprim 100mg PO ON
- **Comments:** Indicated when non-antimicrobial measures (behavioural, hygiene) fail. Continuous
  prophylaxis for 3–12 months, or post-coital prophylaxis (single dose within 2 hours after
  intercourse). Long-term nitrofurantoin carries rare risks: pulmonary toxicity, hepatotoxicity,
  peripheral polyneuropathy.

## 5. Balanitis (second "5" in the source)

### 5.1 Bacterial balanitis
**Aerobic organisms:** Staphylococcus sp.; Streptococcus sp.
Antibiotics only if bacterial balanitis is suspected or evident. For evidence of STI, see the STI section.

- **Preferred**
  - Aerobic infection: Mupirocin ointment 2% LA q12h to q8h for 7–10 days; MAY ADD (severe)
    Amoxicillin/clavulanate 625mg PO q8h for 7 days
  - Anaerobic infection: Amoxicillin/clavulanate 625mg PO q8h for 7 days

### 5.2 Candida balanitis
- **Preferred:** Clotrimazole cream 1% q12h for 7–14 days; OR Miconazole cream 2% q12h for 7–14 days.
  Severe: MAY ADD Fluconazole 200mg PO q24h. If marked inflammation: MAY ADD Hydrocortisone cream 1% q12h.

## 7. Surgical Infections

### 7.1 Renal abscess
#### 7.1.1 Pyonephrosis / perinephric abscess / renal abscess
**Organisms:** E. coli; K. pneumoniae; S. aureus; E. faecalis; S. agalactiae; P. aeruginosa; less
commonly M. tuberculosis.

- **Preferred:** Amoxicillin/clavulanate 1.2g IV q6–8h; OR Ampicillin/sulbactam 3g IV q6h; MAY ADD Gentamicin 5mg/kg IV q24h
- **Alternative:** Cefuroxime 750–1500mg IV q8h; OR Ceftriaxone 2g IV q24h; MAY ADD Gentamicin 5mg/kg IV q24h
- **Comments:** Source control (surgical or pigtail drainage) is the mainstay. 2–3 weeks (IV + oral),
  set by extent of infection, source control, clinical response, imaging resolution and inflammatory
  markers. Step down to oral by culture once afebrile >48 hours, improving and tolerating oral.

### 7.2 Bacterial prostatitis
#### 7.2.1 Acute bacterial prostatitis (ABP)
**Organisms:** E. coli; K. pneumoniae; E. faecalis; P. aeruginosa.

- **Preferred**
  - Outpatient: Ciprofloxacin 500–750mg PO q12h
  - Inpatient: \*Ciprofloxacin 400mg IV q12h; OR Amoxicillin/clavulanate 1.2g IV q6h; OR
    Ampicillin/sulbactam 3g IV q6h; MAY ADD \*\*aminoglycoside
- **Alternative**
  - Outpatient: Trimethoprim/sulfamethoxazole 160/800mg PO q12h
  - Inpatient: Cefuroxime 750–1500mg IV q8h; OR Ceftriaxone 2g IV q24h; OR \*\*Ceftazidime 2g IV q8h
- **Comments:** Obtain urine culture first. Minimum 10–14 days, up to 6 weeks by severity and
  response. Step down to oral once afebrile >48 hours, improving, tolerating oral. STI risk factors →
  STI section. \*PO ciprofloxacin may be considered for non-septic patients who tolerate oral (similar
  bioavailability to IV). \*\*Ceftazidime or aminoglycoside (e.g. amikacin) if at risk of P. aeruginosa.

#### 7.2.2 Chronic bacterial prostatitis (CBP)
Chronic or recurrent urogenital symptoms for at least 3 months.

- **Preferred:** Ciprofloxacin 500–750mg PO q12h for 4–6 weeks
- **Alternative:** Trimethoprim/sulfamethoxazole 160/800mg PO q12h for 4–12 weeks; OR \*Doxycycline
  100mg PO q24h for 10 days; OR \*\*Azithromycin 500mg PO q24h for 3 weeks
- **Comments:** Urology referral recommended to distinguish from chronic non-bacterial prostatitis
  before starting antibiotics. Ciprofloxacin is first line despite high uropathogen resistance because
  of favourable pharmacokinetics. \*Doxycycline only for C. trachomatis or mycoplasma.
  \*\*Azithromycin only for Trichomonas vaginalis.

### 7.3 Testicular / scrotal infection
#### 7.3.1 Epididymo-orchitis (non-STD)
**Organisms:** E. coli; K. pneumoniae; E. faecalis; P. aeruginosa.
- **Preferred:** Levofloxacin 500mg PO q24h for 10 days
- **Alternative:** Ciprofloxacin 500mg PO q12h for 10–14 days; OR Trimethoprim/sulfamethoxazole
  160/800mg PO q12h for 10 days; OR Ofloxacin 200mg PO q12h for 14 days
- **Comments:** STI risk factors or sexually active → STI section.

#### 7.3.2 Testicular / scrotal abscess
- **Preferred:** Amoxicillin/clavulanate 1.2g IV q6–8h; OR Ampicillin/sulbactam 3g IV q6h
- **Alternative:** Cefuroxime 750–1500mg IV q8h; OR Ceftriaxone 2g IV q24h; OR \*Ciprofloxacin 400mg IV q12h
- **Comments:** Surgical drainage is the mainstay. \*Ciprofloxacin 500–750mg PO q12h may be considered
  as outpatient treatment for small abscesses not suitable for drainage.

#### 7.3.3 Fournier's gangrene
**Organisms:** E. coli; K. pneumoniae; E. faecalis; P. aeruginosa; Staphylococcus spp.; anaerobes.
- **Preferred:** \*Piperacillin-tazobactam 4.5g IV q6–8h
- **Alternative:** Ceftriaxone 2g IV q24h PLUS Metronidazole 500mg IV q8h; OR \*\*Meropenem 1g IV q8h;
  OR \*\*Imipenem 500mg IV q6h
- **Comments:** Aggressive surgical debridement is the mainstay; antibiotics until source control.
  \*Pip/tazo q8h: extended infusion over 3–4 hours. \*\*Reserve carbapenems for MDR/ESBL risk.
