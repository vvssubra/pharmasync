## 1. Purulent SSTI
Key principle: incision and drainage.

### 1.1 Folliculitis
**Organisms:** S. aureus; P. aeruginosa; Malassezia furfur; HSV; Demodex spp.

- **1.1.1 Mild:** usually self-limiting, no treatment; good skin hygiene; warm compresses.
- **1.1.2 Known/suspected staphylococcal**
  - Preferred: Topical 2% fusidic acid q8–12h; OR Topical 2% mupirocin q8h (outpatient use only)
  - Comments: warm compress, antiseptic washes, hygiene, clean sharp razors. Send pus/swab for
    culture if accessible. Topical therapy is preferred when persistent but limited in area.
- **1.1.3 Deep or extensive lesions**
  - Preferred: Cloxacillin 500mg PO q6h; OR Cephalexin 1000mg PO q12h
  - Comments: systemic S. aureus cover for deep/extensive folliculitis that fails to resolve or
    recurs after topical therapy. Refer to dermatology if other aetiologies are considered,
    especially if initial systemic treatment fails: (1) gram-negative folliculitis (Enterobacter,
    Klebsiella, E. coli, Serratia, Morganella, Proteus) in patients on long-term antibiotics,
    usually for acne; (2) pseudomonal folliculitis 8–48 hours after contaminated water (hot tub/spa)
    or wet suit exposure; (3) Malassezia, Demodex or HSV in immunocompromised patients.

### 1.2 Furuncles ("boils")
- **Preferred**
  - Mild (localised): Cloxacillin 500mg PO q6h
  - Moderate–severe (extensive/generalised, with or without systemic symptoms, or immunocompromised):
    Amoxicillin/clavulanate 1.2g IV q8h; OR Ampicillin/sulbactam 3g IV q6–8h
- **Alternative:** Mild: Amoxicillin/clavulanate 625mg PO q8h
- **Comments:** 5 days. Try warm compresses and drainage first; antibiotics if these fail.
  Moderate–severe or systemic: manage as carbuncles. Consider IV→PO if improving.

### 1.3 Carbuncles
**Organism:** S. aureus.
- **Preferred:** Cloxacillin 1–2g IV q6h; OR Cefazolin 1–2g IV q6–8h
- **Alternative:** Amoxicillin/clavulanate 1.2g IV q8h; OR Ampicillin/sulbactam 3g IV q6h
- **Comments:** 7–10 days. Surgical drainage is the mainstay. Consider IV→PO with adequate surgical
  treatment and rapid response; targeted therapy once cultures return.

## 2. Non-purulent SSTI

### 2.1 Localised impetigo
**Organisms:** S. aureus; S. pyogenes.
- **Preferred:** Topical 2% fusidic acid q8–12h; OR Topical 2% mupirocin q8h (outpatient only)
- **Alternative:** Cloxacillin 500mg PO q6h; OR Cephalexin 500mg PO q12h
- **Comments:** 5 days.

### 2.2 Generalised impetigo / ecthyma
- **Preferred:** Cloxacillin 500mg PO q6h; OR Cephalexin 1000mg PO q12h.
  Antibiotic allergy: Erythromycin ethylsuccinate 800mg PO q12h
- **Alternative:** Amoxicillin/clavulanate 625mg PO q8h. Other alternative / CA-MRSA:
  Clindamycin 300mg PO q6h; OR Trimethoprim/sulfamethoxazole 160/800mg PO q12h
- **Comments:** 5–7 days by clinical response. Appendix 3.

### 2.3 Erysipelas
**Organism:** S. pyogenes.
- **Preferred:** Phenoxymethylpenicillin 500mg PO q6h; OR Amoxicillin 500mg PO q8h. Severe:
  Benzylpenicillin 2–4MU IV q4–6h. \*CA-MRSA: Clindamycin 300mg PO q6h; OR
  Trimethoprim/sulfamethoxazole 160/800mg PO q12h
- **Alternative:** Cephalexin 1000mg PO q12h. Severe: Cefazolin 1g IV q8h; OR Cefuroxime 750mg IV q8h.
  CA-MRSA: \*\*Vancomycin 15–20mg/kg q8–12h, max 2g/dose
- **Comments:** 5 days. \*IV vancomycin for CA-MRSA when oral therapy is not feasible.
  \*\*Loading dose per Appendix 1.

### 2.4 Ecthyma gangrenosum
Most often Pseudomonas; tailor to susceptibility.
- **Preferred:** Ciprofloxacin 400mg IV q12h; OR \*Piperacillin/tazobactam 4.5g IV q6–8h
- **Alternative:** Ceftazidime 2g IV q8h; OR Cefepime 2g IV q8h
- **Comments:** 7 days by response. IV→PO once improved (Appendix 6). \*Pip/tazo q8h: extended infusion over 3–4 hours.

### 2.5 Cellulitis
**Organisms (all grades):** S. pyogenes; S. aureus.
- Mild: no systemic signs. Moderate: SIRS without hypotension. Severe: SIRS with hypotension.
- SIRS: fever, tachypnoea, tachycardia, abnormal white cell count.

- **Preferred**
  - Mild: Cloxacillin 500mg PO q6h; OR Cephalexin 1000mg PO q12h; OR Amoxicillin 500mg PO q8h
  - Moderate: Cloxacillin 1–2g IV q6h; OR Cefazolin 1–2g IV q8h
  - Severe: \*Piperacillin/tazobactam 4.5g IV q6–8h (de-escalate once cultures available / necrotising fasciitis ruled out)
  - \*\*Prophylaxis: Phenoxymethylpenicillin 250mg PO q12h (500mg PO q12h if BMI ≥33)
- **Alternative**
  - Mild: Amoxicillin/clavulanate 625mg PO q8h; OR Ampicillin/sulbactam 375mg PO q12h; OR Cefuroxime 500mg PO q12h
  - Moderate: Ampicillin/sulbactam 3g IV q6h; OR Amoxicillin/clavulanate 1.2g IV q8h; OR Cefuroxime 750mg–1.5g IV q8h
  - Severe: Ampicillin/sulbactam 3g IV q6h; OR Amoxicillin/clavulanate 1.2g IV q8h
  - Antibiotic allergy: Clindamycin 600mg IV q6h (de-escalate as above)
  - Prophylaxis with antibiotic allergy: Clarithromycin 250mg PO q24h; OR Erythromycin ethylsuccinate 400mg PO q12h
- **Comments:** 5–10 days (longer if no improvement or underlying medical condition). IV→PO when
  improved (Appendix 6); Appendix 3 for allergy. \*Pip/tazo q8h: extended infusion over 3–4 hours.
  \*\*Prophylaxis may be considered for 3–4 episodes/year despite treating predisposing factors
  (lymphoedema, obesity, inflammatory dermatoses, tinea pedis, venous insufficiency), for 6–12 months,
  then reassess.

### 2.6 Bite-related infections
**Organisms:** dog/cat — Pasteurella multocida, Capnocytophaga canimorsus; human — Eikenella
corrodens, anaerobes, S. aureus; monkey — Streptococcus, S. aureus, anaerobes, \*Cercopithecine
herpesvirus 1 (macaque exposure); rodent — Francisella tularensis, Leptospira, Pasteurella,
Spirillum minor; reptile — anaerobes, Enterobacterales.

**Consider prophylaxis for:** human bite; cat bite; presentation >8 hours; deep puncture near bone/joint;
crush injury; immunocompromised patients.
**All bites:** thorough irrigation and/or debridement is the priority; exploration and washout for
any broken skin. Consider plastic surgery for face, hands, feet, genitalia. Assess tetanus risk
(Guidelines for Adult Immunisation). Rabies: MOH rabies management guideline.

- **Preferred**
  - Dog/cat/human/rodent/reptile: Amoxicillin/clavulanate 625mg PO q8h or 1.2g IV q8h
  - Monkey: the above PLUS \*Acyclovir 800mg PO 5 times daily for 14 days
- **Alternative**
  - Antibiotic allergy: Cefuroxime 750mg–1.5g IV q8h; OR Ceftriaxone 2g IV q24h, PLUS Metronidazole 500mg IV q8h
  - Oral: Doxycycline 200mg PO q24h Day 1 then 100–200mg PO q24h; OR Cefuroxime 500mg PO q12h PLUS Metronidazole 400mg PO q8h
- **Comments:** Prophylaxis 3 days; treatment 5 days. \*Add acyclovir only for macaque exposure via
  bites/scratches, tissue culture material, autopsy tissue, needlestick, cage scratches or mucosal splash.

### 2.7 Cat scratch disease (Bartonella henselae)
- **Preferred:** Azithromycin 500mg PO Day 1, then 250mg PO q24h for 4 days

### 2.8 Sea water exposure (Vibrio sp.)
- **Preferred:** Doxycycline 200mg STAT, then 100mg PO q12h; MAY ADD \*Ceftriaxone 2g IV q24h
- **Comments:** \*Consider a third-generation cephalosporin in severe infection.

### 2.9 Fresh or brackish water exposure
**Organisms:** Aeromonas; Plesiomonas; S. aureus; S. pyogenes.
- **Preferred:** Trimethoprim/sulfamethoxazole 320/1600mg PO q12h
- **Alternative:** Ciprofloxacin 400mg IV q12h; OR Ciprofloxacin 750mg PO q12h PLUS Cloxacillin
  500mg PO q6h; MAY ADD \*Metronidazole 400mg PO q8h
- **Comments:** Oral therapy encouraged if no systemic features or deeper tissue involvement. Add
  metronidazole for soil- or sewage-contaminated water.

### 2.10 Neutropenic patients
**Organisms:** P. aeruginosa; other gram-negatives.
- **Preferred:** \*Piperacillin/tazobactam 4.5g IV q6–8h; OR Cefepime 2g IV q8h
- **Comments:** \*Pip/tazo q8h: extended infusion over 3–4 hours.

### 2.11 MRSA SSTI
- **Preferred:** Vancomycin 15–20mg/kg IV q8–12h. Severe: load 25–30mg/kg IV, then 15–20mg/kg
  (actual body weight) IV q8–12h; max 2g/dose.
- **Alternative:** Linezolid 600mg IV/PO q12h. By culture/susceptibility: Clindamycin 300–600mg
  IV/PO q6–8h; OR Trimethoprim/sulfamethoxazole 160/800mg PO q12h; OR Doxycycline 100mg PO q12h
- **Comments:** IV→PO when improved (Appendix 6); vancomycin loading per Appendix 1.
  **Decolonisation:** mupirocin 2% to both nostrils; chlorhexidine 4% bath or 2% wipe (especially
  axilla, groin, perineum). **Consider CA-MRSA if:** known CA-MRSA outbreak; non-resolving cellulitis.

## 3. Peripheral Phlebitis / Thrombophlebitis
**Organisms:** S. aureus; coagulase-negative Staphylococcus; gram-negatives.

- Grade 1: observe cannula. Grade 2: resite/remove cannula. Grade 3–4: remove cannula, take blood
  culture, consider treatment. Grade 5: blood culture, resite/remove cannula, start treatment.
- **Preferred (Grade 3–5):** Cloxacillin 1g IV q6h. If blood culture negative and improving, switch
  to Cloxacillin 500mg PO q6h or Cephalexin 1000mg PO q12h.
- **Alternative (Grade 3–5):** Cefazolin 2g IV q8h
- **Comments:** 5 days by response. Remove peripheral IV catheters with pain, induration, erythema or exudate.

**Visual Infusion Phlebitis score:** 0 healthy site; 1 one of slight pain/slight redness; 2 two of
pain/redness/swelling; 3 pain along cannula path + redness + swelling; 4 as 3 + palpable venous
cord; 5 as 4 + febrile.

## 4. Pressure Injury (bed sore / decubitus ulcer)
Polymicrobial. Mainstay is prevention. Management depends on local assessment and NPIAP staging.

- **Preferred (if systemic infection):** Ampicillin/sulbactam 3g IV q6h; OR Amoxicillin/clavulanate 1.2g IV q8h
- **Comments:** Consider other sources of infection and escalate accordingly. Oral antibiotics may be
  considered for localised infection. IV→PO when improved (Appendix 6).

## 5. Mycobacterial Infections

### 5.1 Hansen's disease (leprosy, M. leprae)
- **Preferred:** Rifampicin 600mg PO once monthly (supervised) PLUS Dapsone 100mg PO q24h PLUS
  Clofazimine 300mg PO monthly and 50mg PO q24h. Duration: paucibacillary 6 months; multibacillary 1 year.
- **Alternative (\*resistance or hypersensitivity to first line):** substitute one of Ofloxacin
  400mg PO q24h; Minocycline 100mg PO q24h; Clarithromycin 500mg PO q24h.
- **Comments:** \*Second-line / drug-resistant regimens only by a dermatologist. Contact prophylaxis
  after excluding leprosy and TB: single-dose rifampicin — age 10–14: 450mg; ≥15: 600mg.

### 5.2 NTM skin and soft tissue infections
Treatment is individualised by susceptibility and site; usually 2–3 agents for 6–12 months. NTM are
intrinsically resistant to many antibiotics; susceptibility testing at reference laboratories;
selection is often empirical per guideline.

#### 5.2.1 M. marinum, M. kansasii, M. fortuitum/chelonae/abscessus complex
- **Preferred:** Clarithromycin 500mg PO q12h OR Azithromycin 500mg PO q24h, PLUS Rifampicin 600mg
  PO q24h OR Ethambutol 25mg/kg PO q24h OR Doxycycline 100mg PO q12h
- **Alternative:** Amikacin 15mg/kg IV q24h
- **Comments:** 6–12 months or until clinical resolution.

#### 5.2.3 M. ulcerans (Buruli ulcer)
- **Preferred:** Rifampicin 10mg/kg PO q24h PLUS Clarithromycin 7.5mg/kg PO q12h
- **Alternative:** Rifampicin 10mg/kg PO q24h PLUS Streptomycin 15mg/kg IM q24h for 4 weeks, then
  Rifampicin plus Clarithromycin as above
- **Comments:** Wide surgical excision and debridement are important. WHO category 1 (pre-ulcerative,
  lesion <5cm): 4 weeks antibiotics with simple excision or 8 weeks antibiotics alone. Category 2:
  at least 8 weeks plus supportive care (e.g. surgery). Category 3 (>15cm): antibiotics plus
  debridement and reconstruction; duration by response (joint/bone complications common).

## 6. Fungal Infections
**Itraconazole note (applies throughout):** absorption depends on gut acidity — take capsule with
food and an acidic drink (e.g. cola, orange juice); avoid PPIs and H2 blockers.

### 6.1 Tinea capitis (Trichophyton, Microsporum)
- **Preferred:** Griseofulvin 500mg PO q12h for 6–12 weeks or until cultures negative; OR Terbinafine
  250mg PO q24h; PLUS 2.5% selenium sulphide shampoo OR 2% ketoconazole shampoo 2–3×/week for 2 weeks
- **Alternative:** \*Itraconazole 5mg/kg PO q24h; OR Fluconazole 6mg/kg PO q24h.
  Duration by mycology/agent/response (e.g. Trichophyton 2–4 weeks; Microsporum 8–12 weeks).
- **Comments:** Kerion: griseofulvin first line unless Trichophyton cultured; treatment may be longer.
  Contacts may use 2% ketoconazole shampoo 2–3×/week for 2 weeks. Avoid surgical excision. Topical
  therapy alone is not recommended. Consider oral prednisolone in selected cases.

### 6.2 Tinea barbae
Same as tinea capitis.

### 6.3 Tinea corporis / cruris / faciei (Trichophyton, Microsporum, Epidermophyton)
- **Preferred**
  - Mild: topical imidazole/allylamine cream/lotion q12h — clotrimazole 1%, miconazole 2%,
    ketoconazole 2%, or terbinafine 1% — until clearance plus 2 further weeks
  - Extensive: Terbinafine 250mg PO q24h for 2 weeks; OR \*Itraconazole 200mg PO q24h for 2 weeks;
    OR Griseofulvin 500mg PO q12h or q24h for 4–6 weeks
- **Alternative (extensive):** Fluconazole 100mg daily for 1–2 weeks; OR Fluconazole 200mg weekly for 3–4 weeks
- **Comments:** Caution with systemic antifungals in renal/hepatic impairment; terbinafine clearance
  is reduced in renal impairment (prefer others). Topical nystatin is ineffective against
  dermatophytes. Adjust per culture/susceptibility.

### 6.4 Tinea manuum / pedis
- **Preferred:** First line: topical antifungals as above for 4–8 weeks. Resistant cases: Terbinafine
  250mg PO q24h for 2–4 weeks; OR \*Itraconazole 200mg PO q24h for 2–4 weeks; OR Griseofulvin 500mg PO q12h for 6–12 weeks
- **Alternative (resistant):** Fluconazole 150mg/week PO for 4 weeks
- **Comments:** Topical keratolytics for hyperkeratotic type. Potassium permanganate 1:10,000 wet
  dressings for 20 minutes 2–3×/day if vesiculation or maceration. Systemic antifungals first line
  for severe moccasin-type tinea pedis or severe recurrent tinea with blisters.

### 6.5 Tinea unguium (onychomycosis)
- **Preferred:** \*Amorolfine 5% nail lacquer weekly (fingernails 6 months; toenails 12 months); OR
  \*\*Pulse itraconazole 200mg PO q12h for 1 week per month (fingernails 2 months; toenails 3 months);
  OR Terbinafine 250mg PO q24h (fingernails 6 weeks; toenails 12 weeks)
- **Alternative:** Griseofulvin 500mg PO q12h (fingernails 6 months; toenails 12 months); OR
  Fluconazole 150mg PO weekly (fingernails ≥3 months; toenails 6–12 months)
- **Comments:** \*Amorolfine not for children <12. Topical agents if systemic contraindicated.
  Confirm diagnosis with KOH, culture or PAS stain; empirical treatment not recommended.

### 6.6 Tinea versicolor (Malassezia furfur / Pityrosporum orbiculare)
- **Preferred:** Topical only first line — selenium sulphide 2% shampoo (apply 10 minutes before
  bathing, or dilute 1:1 and leave overnight, 1–2 weeks); OR ketoconazole 2% shampoo 10 minutes before
  bathing. Face: topical imidazole 4–6 weeks (miconazole 2% or clotrimazole 1% cream q12h).
  Recurrent/resistant: \*Itraconazole 200mg PO q24h for 1 week or 400mg single dose; OR Fluconazole 300mg PO weekly for 2 weeks
- **Alternative:** Sulfur preparation; OR salicylic solution
- **Comments:** Ketoconazole or selenium sulphide shampoo every 2–4 weeks for about 6 months may prevent recurrence.

### 6.7 Candidiasis (Candida albicans)
- **Preferred:** Mild: topical imidazole q12h until clear (miconazole 2% / clotrimazole 1% cream).
  Extensive: \*Itraconazole 200mg PO q24h for 1 week
- **Alternative (extensive):** Fluconazole 100mg PO q24h for 1 week (severe/immunocompromised)
- **Comments:** Treat sexual partner in recurrent infection.

### 6.8 Subcutaneous fungal infections (lymphocutaneous/cutaneous sporotrichosis, chromoblastomycosis)
- **Preferred:** \*Itraconazole 200mg PO q12h until all lesions resolve (usually 3–6 months)
- **Alternative (cannot tolerate itraconazole):** Terbinafine 500mg PO q12h; OR Fluconazole 400mg q24h
- **Comments:** Longer treatment may be needed in immunocompromise (e.g. AIDS) — see Immunocompromised
  section. \*\*Avoid azoles in pregnancy.

### 6.9 Systemic sporotrichosis (pulmonary, osteoarticular, meningeal, disseminated)
- **Preferred:** Amphotericin B deoxycholate 0.7–1mg/kg IV q24h for 2 weeks; OR \*Amphotericin B
  (lipid) 3–5mg/kg q24h for 2 weeks; followed by \*\*Itraconazole 200mg PO q12–24h for at least 12
  months. \*\*\*In pregnancy/breastfeeding: Terbinafine 500mg PO q12h.
- **Alternative (\*\*pregnancy/breastfeeding):** Amphotericin B deoxycholate or lipid formulation as above
- **Comments:** Longer therapy in immunocompromise (Immunocompromised section). \*Not in MOH Drug
  Formulary. \*\*\*Avoid azoles in pregnancy.

### 6.10 Cutaneous fungal infection in immunocompromised patients
See disseminated fungal infection in the Immunocompromised section. Skin biopsy for HPE and culture
before starting treatment.

### 6.11 Aspergillus, Scedosporium apiospermum, Lomentospora prolificans, Fusarium
- **Preferred:** Voriconazole 6mg/kg IV q12h for 2 doses, then 4mg/kg IV q12h
- **Alternative:** Amphotericin B deoxycholate 0.7–1mg/kg q24h; OR \*Amphotericin B (lipid) 3–5mg/kg q24h
- **Comments:** Lomentospora: consider surgical debridement and reducing immunosuppression. IV→PO when
  improving and tolerating oral. \*Not in MOH Drug Formulary.

### 6.12 Cryptococcal skin infections
- **Preferred:** Fluconazole 400mg PO q24h

### 6.13 Penicilliosis, disseminated histoplasmosis, disseminated cryptococcosis
See disseminated fungal infection in the Immunocompromised section (HIV opportunistic infections).

## 7. Viral Infections

### 7.1 Herpes simplex
- **7.1.1 Non-genitalia:** Acyclovir 400mg PO q8h; alternative \*Valacyclovir 1g PO q12h or
  \*Famciclovir 500mg PO q12–24h. 7 days. Trunk/limb vesiculation: potassium permanganate 1:10,000
  wet dressings 20 minutes 2–3×/day; head/neck: normal saline wet dressings 20 minutes 2–3×/day.
  \*Not in MOH Drug Formulary.
- **7.1.2 Genitalia:** see STI section.

### 7.2 Varicella zoster (chickenpox)
- **Preferred:** Immunocompetent: Acyclovir 800mg PO 5 times daily for 7 days. Immunocompromised:
  Acyclovir 10mg/kg IV q8h for 7 days (oral once improving).
- **Comments:** Start early, within 48 hours. Wet dressings as in 7.1.

### 7.3 Herpes zoster
- **Preferred:** as for varicella zoster.
- **Comments:** Topical antivirals not recommended. Systemic antivirals for all immunocompromised
  patients, and for immunocompetent patients who are >50 years, have moderate–severe pain,
  moderate–severe rash, or non-truncal involvement. Start within 48–72 hours. Wet dressings as in 7.1.

## 8. Parasitic Infestation

### 8.1 Scabies (Sarcoptes scabiei)
- **Preferred:** Benzyl benzoate emulsion 25% neck down, leave 24 hours, for 2–3 days; OR permethrin 5%
  lotion/cream left 8 hours. Repeat after 1 week. Pregnancy/immunocompromised: permethrin 5% for 8
  hours, repeat after 1 week.
- **Comments:** Wash clothing and bedding in hot water and dry.

### 8.2 Head lice (Pediculus humanus capitis)
- **Preferred:** Permethrin 1% lotion to scalp 10 minutes, wash off; OR malathion 1% shampoo 15 minutes,
  wash off. Repeat after 1 week.
- **Comments:** Fine-toothed nit comb to reduce re-infestation.

### 8.3 Body lice / pubic lice (Pediculus humanus)
- **Preferred:** Malathion 0.5% lotion for 8–12 hours, wash off; OR permethrin 1% cream to affected area 10 minutes, wash off.

## 9. Surgical Infections

### 9.1 Breast infections
- **9.1.1 Breast abscess / mastitis (S. aureus)**
  - Preferred: Cloxacillin 1–2g IV q6h; OR Cefazolin 1–2g IV q8h
  - Alternative: Amoxicillin/clavulanate 625mg PO q8h; OR Ampicillin/sulbactam 750mg PO q12h.
    Antibiotic allergy: Clindamycin 600mg IV/PO q8h
  - Comments: Surgical drainage is the mainstay. 10–14 days; 5–7 days if adequate source control and
    clinical improvement. Appendix 3.
- **9.1.2 Other complicated breast infections (e.g. foreign-body related):** empirical antibiotics not
  recommended; early referral to primary/plastic surgery.

### 9.2 Burn-related infections
Burn wound is sterile up to 48 hours; not all burns need antibiotics; ideal care in a burn unit;
consider plastic surgery input; consider prophylaxis for severe burns and high-risk groups.
- **9.2.1 Severe burn and high-risk group**
  - Preferred: Cefazolin 1–2g IV q8h
  - Alternative: Amoxicillin/clavulanate 1.2g IV q8h; OR Ampicillin/sulbactam 3g IV q6–8h
  - High-risk groups: inhalation injury; immunocompromise (extremes of age, diabetes, obesity,
    impaired immunity); delayed burn excision; mechanical ventilation; TBSA >20%

### 9.3 Orofacial-related infections
- **9.3.1 Odontogenic:** see Oral/Dental section.
- **9.3.2 Non-odontogenic (facial cellulitis/abscess):** Amoxicillin/clavulanate 1.2g IV q8h;
  allergy: Clindamycin 600mg IV/PO q8h. Surgical drainage ± debridement is the mainstay; deep neck
  space abscess → ENT section.
- **9.3.3 Cartilage infections (pinna, nose):** Preferred Amoxicillin/clavulanate 1.2g IV q8h;
  alternative Ampicillin/sulbactam 3g IV q6–8h; cartilage penetration: Ciprofloxacin 400mg IV BD or
  500mg PO BD. 5–7 days. Malignant otitis externa → ENT section.
