## 1. Throat and Upper Respiratory Tract

### 1.1 Tonsillitis / Pharyngitis (sore throat)
**Organisms:** respiratory virus (>80%); Group A Streptococcus (GAS).
Use the Modified Centor Score to decide whether antibiotics are needed.

- **Score <3:** no antibiotics (usually viral); symptomatic treatment only (analgesics, antipyretics).
- **Score 3–5:** antibiotics (higher GAS risk).
  - **Preferred:** Phenoxymethylpenicillin (Pen V) 500mg PO q6h or 1g PO q12h; OR Benzathine penicillin 1.2MU IM single dose
  - **Alternative:** Amoxicillin 500mg PO q8h
  - **Antibiotic allergy:** Erythromycin ethylsuccinate 800mg q12h
  - **Comments:** 5–10 days. Consider 10 days where rheumatic fever is highly prevalent, or in
    patients aged 3–21 with a history of rheumatic fever / rheumatic heart disease. Allergy → Appendix 3.

### 1.2 Acute peritonsillar abscess (quinsy)
**Organisms:** Group A Streptococcus; Fusobacterium necrophorum; Streptococcus anginosus group.
Drainage or aspiration is the mainstay.

- **Preferred:** Benzylpenicillin 2MU IV q6h PLUS Metronidazole 500mg IV q6–8h; OR
  Ampicillin/sulbactam 3g IV q6h; OR Amoxicillin/clavulanate 1.2g IV q8h
- **Alternative (antibiotic allergy):** Clindamycin 600–900mg IV q8h
- **Comments:** 10–14 days (IV + oral). Appendix 6 for IV→PO; Appendix 3 for allergy.

### 1.3 Diphtheria
**Organism:** Corynebacterium diphtheriae.

- **Preferred:**
  - \*Erythromycin lactobionate 500mg IV q6h, then \*\*Erythromycin ethylsuccinate 800mg PO q6h; OR
  - \*Benzylpenicillin 25,000 units/kg q6h (max 1MU IV/IM q6h), then Pen V 500mg PO q6h
  - PLUS Diphtheria antitoxin
- **Alternative:** \*\*\*Azithromycin 500mg OD IV/PO PLUS Diphtheria antitoxin
  (\*\*\*no CLSI/EUCAST breakpoint; only for patients who cannot tolerate penicillin or erythromycin)
- **Comments**
  - 14 days (IV + oral). Antibiotics do not substitute for antitoxin; their role is to eradicate
    the organism and stop further toxin production.
  - \*IV→PO: parenteral preferred when unable to swallow; switch once swallowing comfortably (Appendix 6).
  - \*\*Erythromycin base 250mg = erythromycin ethylsuccinate 400mg.
  - **Close contacts:** Benzathine penicillin 1.2MU IM single dose; OR Erythromycin ethylsuccinate
    800mg q6h for 7–10 days; OR \*\*\*Azithromycin 500mg OD for 7–10 days.
  - **Antitoxin dose by severity:** pharyngeal/laryngeal disease of 2 days 20,000–40,000 units;
    nasopharyngeal 40,000–60,000 units; systemic disease ≥3 days or diffuse neck swelling
    80,000–120,000 units. Give over 60 minutes.

### 1.4 Acute epiglottitis (supraglottitis)
**Organisms:** S. pneumoniae; Group A Streptococcus; Haemophilus influenzae type B.

- **Preferred:** Ceftriaxone 2g IV q24h; OR Ampicillin/sulbactam 3g IV q6h; OR Amoxicillin/clavulanate 1.2g IV q8h
- **Alternative (antibiotic allergy):** Clindamycin 600–900mg IV q8h PLUS Ciprofloxacin 400mg IV q12h
- **Comments:** 7–10 days (IV + oral); longer for bacteraemia, concomitant meningitis or
  immunocompromise. Needs urgent hospitalisation (risk of life-threatening airway obstruction,
  especially in children). Consider adding vancomycin for moderate–severe sepsis, concomitant
  meningitis or prior MRSA colonisation. Appendix 3 / Appendix 6.

### 1.5 Deep neck space / parapharyngeal / retropharyngeal abscess / Ludwig angina
**Organisms (usually polymicrobial):** anaerobes (Fusobacterium, Prevotella); Streptococcus spp.; S. aureus.

- **Preferred:** Ampicillin/sulbactam 3g IV q6h; OR Amoxicillin/clavulanate 1.2g IV q8h; OR
  Cefuroxime 1.5g IV q8h PLUS Metronidazole 500mg IV q6h
- **Alternative (penicillin allergy)**
  - Immediate / delayed non-severe: Cefazolin 2g IV q8h OR Ceftriaxone 2g IV q24h, PLUS Metronidazole 500mg IV q6h
  - Immediate / delayed severe: Clindamycin 600–900mg IV q8h
- **Comments:** 10–14 days (IV + oral). Surgical drainage/debridement for significant abscess.
  IV clindamycin may be added if necrotising fasciitis is evident. Switch to oral after at least
  3–5 days IV once improved (Appendix 6).

## 2. Nose

### 2.1 Acute rhinosinusitis (ARS)
Mostly viral (bacterial 0.5–2%); antibiotics are not needed in the great majority of cases —
most are self-limited complications of viral URTI. Use the source's algorithm to separate
bacterial from viral (algorithm not reproduced here).

### 2.2 Acute bacterial rhinosinusitis (ABRS)
**Organisms:** S. pneumoniae; H. influenzae; Moraxella catarrhalis.

- **Preferred:** Amoxicillin 500–1000mg PO q8h; OR Amoxicillin/clavulanate 625mg PO q8h.
  \*If no improvement after 3 days of oral antibiotic, refer to Otorhinolaryngology.
- **Alternative (penicillin allergy):** Doxycycline 100mg q12h. Pregnant with antibiotic allergy:
  Azithromycin 500mg PO q24h for 3 days.
- **Comments:** Duration 5 days. Appendix 3.

## 3. Ear

### 3.1 Acute otitis media (AOM)
See Section C2, Clinical Pathway in Primary Care — Acute Otitis Media.

### 3.2 Malignant / necrotising otitis externa
**Organisms:** P. aeruginosa (95%); S. aureus.

- **Preferred:** Ceftazidime 2g IV q8h; OR \*Piperacillin/tazobactam 4.5g IV q6–8h
- **Alternative:** Ciprofloxacin 400mg IV q8h (beta-lactam allergy)
- **Comments:** 6 weeks (IV + oral). On clinical response, consider Ciprofloxacin 750mg PO q12h to
  complete 6 weeks. \*Pip/tazo q8h: extended infusion over 3–4 hours.

### 3.3 Acute localised otitis externa
**Organisms:** S. aureus; S. pyogenes.

- **Preferred:** Cloxacillin 500mg PO q6h; OR Cephalexin 500mg PO q6h
- **Alternative (penicillin allergy):** Clindamycin 600mg PO q8h
- **Comments:** 5 days.

### 3.4 Acute diffuse otitis externa (swimmer's ear)
**Organisms:** P. aeruginosa; S. aureus.

- **Preferred:** Ofloxacin 0.3% otic solution, 10 drops into affected ear(s) q24h.
  MAY ADD steroid ear drops (when fungal infection is NOT suspected).
- **Comments:** Aural toileting for discharging ears. 7 days.

### 3.5 Chronic suppurative otitis media
**Organisms (often polymicrobial):** anaerobes; S. aureus; Enterobacterales; P. aeruginosa; M. tuberculosis.

- **Preferred:** Ofloxacin 0.3% otic solution, 10 drops into affected ear(s) q12h. ENT referral
  recommended to exclude cholesteatoma or chronic osteitis.
- **Comments:** Aural toileting for discharging ears. 10–14 days.

### 3.6 Otomycosis
**Organisms:** Candida sp.; Aspergillus sp.

- **Preferred:** Clotrimazole 1% ear solution, apply q6–8h
- **Comments:** Aural toileting required. 10–14 days.
